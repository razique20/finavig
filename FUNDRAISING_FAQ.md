# FUNDRAISING_FAQ.md — the honest answers before you raise

Plain-language answers to the questions every first-time founder asks.
Written for Finavig (GCC, solo founder, live app, pre-revenue), with real
numbers. Companion file: `FUNDRAISING.md` (the 100-investor list).

> Not legal or financial advice. For anything you sign, use a lawyer —
> GCC startup lawyers are affordable for standard SAFE/term-sheet work.

---

## Q1. When should I start raising — right after Play Store / App Store launch, or after getting users?

**Short answer: not immediately after launch. Start applications now,
start investor meetings after first real usage.**

Why:

| Timing | What investors see | Your position |
|---|---|---|
| Before launch | A promise | Weakest. Only friends & family / accelerators |
| **Week 1–4 after launch** | A product exists | Better, but "no data yet" — raise only small money |
| **2–3 months after launch** | Usage, retention, first payments | **This is the sweet spot for pre-seed** |
| 12+ months, growing | Revenue | Seed round territory — best terms |

**The Finavig-specific trigger checklist — start talking to investors when you have:**

- [ ] App live on both stores, crash-free
- [ ] **100–300 real users** (not downloads — users who return)
- [ ] **D30 retention data** (even if it's 20–30% — investors respect real numbers)
- [ ] **10+ business collections** created (proves the AED 99 Business tier interest)
- [ ] 5–10 users who say "yes I'd pay AED 25 for this" (survey is fine)
- [ ] 2–3 short screenshots/videos of the app doing something impressive

**But do these IN PARALLEL, starting today:**
1. Apply to **equity-free programs** (Hub71, Sheraa, TAQADAM, in5) — these are application-based, not negotiation-based, and give money + credibility with **zero dilution**
2. Apply to **accelerators** (Flat6Labs, Antler Dubai) — cohort timing is fixed; apply early
3. Keep building usage — every week of traction raises your valuation before you even start

**One exception:** if a *strategic* angel (someone with GCC SME customers or
fintech experience) offers money at launch — take the small cheque. But
don't spend 3 months "raising" with zero users; that's the worst use of
your time and gives you the worst valuation.

## Q2. If a person or company invests in us — what does it really mean?

**It means you sell a small piece of your company for cash. Five things
change:**

**1. They own a percentage of your company.**
Invest $300K at a $3M valuation → they own 10% forever (until dilution).
You still own 90%. You are still the boss at pre-seed/seed.

**2. The money goes to the company, not to you.**
This surprises many first-time founders. The $300K lands in the company
bank account and is spent on the business. Your personal income = a
modest **founder salary** the company pays you (often $0–1,500/month at
pre-seed, more after seed).

**3. They get rights — usually these:**
- **Information rights:** monthly/quarterly updates (metrics, spending)
- **Board seat:** sometimes at pre-seed, almost always at seed (you keep majority)
- **Veto rights on big things:** selling the company, issuing more shares cheaply
- **Pro-rata rights:** the right to buy more in the next round to keep their %

**4. They expect a return — through an "exit".**
Investors make money when the company is **acquired** or **goes public**.
They are not your bank; they're partners who want the company to grow
10×. If the company fails, they usually lose their money and you owe
them **nothing** (as long as you have a proper limited company and no
personal guarantees — never sign a personal guarantee).

**5. You gain obligations — and benefits.**
Obligations: reports, spending discipline, hitting milestones.
Benefits: money, advice, their network, credibility with the next investor.

**What it does NOT mean:** they don't control your daily work, they can't
take your shares back if you perform (vesting applies to *your* shares
over time — normal), and they can't fire you without board process.

## Q3. What valuation should I tell them?

**Learn two words first:**

- **Pre-money valuation** = what the company is worth *before* the money comes in
- **Post-money** = pre-money + the investment

Example: raise **$300K** at **$2.7M pre-money** →
post-money = **$3M** → investor owns **300K ÷ 3M = 10%**.

**Realistic GCC ranges (2026):**

| Stage | Your state | Valuation range | What you give away |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed, no users | Just launched | $1M–$2M pre-money | 15–25% |
| **Pre-seed, early users** | **Finavig's target** | **$2M–$3.5M pre-money** | **10–20%** |
| Seed | 1–5k users, some revenue | $4M–$8M post-money | 15–25% |
| Series A | Real revenue (ARR), proven growth | $10M–$30M | 15–25% |

**For Finavig, say this:** *"I'm raising $300K on a SAFE with a $3M
valuation cap."* If they push back, $2.5M cap is still fine. Never start
below $2M — it signals desperation.

**Rules of thumb:**
- Your first cheque's valuation is mostly about **the team + traction story**, not a formula
- Don't inflate it. A too-high valuation now = a "down round" (lower price) later = bad signal and angry early investors
- The **cap** on a SAFE is the maximum price your early money converts at; it's the normal way pre-seed is done in MENA
- If you have zero users, expect $1–2M. That's why Q1 says: get users first

## Q4. What is seed, Series A, B, C…? (the stages explained)

Think of it as **levels of proof**. Each level = more money, more proof,
less equity for you.

| Stage | What you've proven | Typical raise (GCC) | Who invests | Dilution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bootstrapped** | Nothing — self funded | $0 | You, revenue, grants | 0% |
| **Pre-seed** | Product exists, first users | $150K–$500K | Angels, micro-VC, accelerators | 10–20% |
| **Seed** | People use it and return; some pay | $500K–$3M | Seed VC funds (Shorooq, BECO…) | 15–25% |
| **Series A** | Repeatable revenue (~$1M+ ARR), unit economics work | $3M–$10M | Bigger VCs (Global Ventures, STV…) | 15–25% |
| **Series B** | Scaling what works; market leadership in sight | $10M–$30M+ | Growth funds, sovereign money | 10–20% |
| **Series C and beyond** | Expansion, acquisitions, pre-IPO | $30M+ | Late-stage, PE, sovereign | 10–20% |

"Series" is just a naming convention (A = first institutional round,
B = second…). **You don't have to do all of them** — plenty of GCC
companies stop at seed + revenue and grow on their own money.

**What matters for Finavig now:** your realistic path is
**accelerator/grant → pre-seed ($150–400K) → seed ($750K–1.5M after
traction)**. Series A is 2–3 years away at best. Don't pitch above your
stage — it wastes meetings.

## Q5. If I take investment, will my share get reduced?

**Yes — this is called dilution, and it's normal and mathematical.**

Real example with a solo founder:

| Event | You own | Investors own |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 100% | — |
| Pre-seed: $300K at $3M post | **90%** | 10% |
| ESOP pool created for hires (10%) | **80%** | 10% + 10% team pool |
| Seed: $1M at $6M post (~17%) | **~66%** | ~34% total |
| Series A: $5M at $20M post (25%) | **~50%** | ~50% total |

**Key truths:**
- **Dilution is not losing.** 66% of a $6M company ($4M) beats 100% of a company worth $0
- **You keep control** as long as you + friendly board hold the majority. Founders normally control through Series A
- **Pro-rata rights** let you (and investors) put more money in later rounds to protect your %
- **Never give more than 25% in any single round** at your stage — if an investor asks for 40%, walk away
- Watch the **ESOP pool**: investors often ask you to create it *before* the round, so it dilutes only you — negotiate it as part of the round so everyone shares it

## Q6. Can I use the funding for licences in all GCC countries, offices, team, etc.?

**Yes — funding can legally pay for all of that. But investors judge HOW you
spend, and at pre-seed there's a right order.**

**What investors want the money spent on (in order):**
1. **Growth** — marketing, sales, getting users (they fund growth, not comfort)
2. **Product** — building what customers pay for
3. **Team** — hires that unlock growth (part-time contractors first)
4. **Legal/compliance** — the minimum needed to operate and get paid
5. **Offices** — last. Remote-first is expected at this stage

**Your specific ideas, judged:**

| Plan | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Licences in **all 6 GCC countries** now | ❌ Too early | Each licence costs money + months of admin. Start UAE, add KSA when revenue is there |
| **UAE entity** (freezone, ~AED 15–30K/yr) | ✅ Yes | Investors need something to invest into; you need a bank account |
| **KSA entity** (MISA licence) | ⏳ Later | Add when you have Saudi customers or Saudi investors requiring it |
| **Office** | ⚠️ Only if needed | A small co-working desk is fine; a fancy office is a red flag |
| **Team** | ✅ Yes, carefully | Hire for growth roles (growth, support, sales). Not a big fixed payroll |
| **PRO/renewal services** (the concierge idea) | ⏳ Later | Services business — do it after PMF, per MONETIZATION.md |

**Typical pre-seed budget split ($300K example):**
- Product & engineering: ~40% ($120K — can include your modest salary)
- Growth & marketing: ~30% ($90K)
- Legal, accounting, licences, banking: ~10% ($30K)
- Buffer: ~20% ($60K)

**The "use of funds" slide in your deck is a promise.** Investors won't
sue you for spending differently, but they will remember it at the next
round — and the next round is always led by people who ask current
investors about you.

**Golden rule:** every spend should answer *"does this make users or
revenue grow faster?"* If no — don't spend it yet.

## Q7. Related questions you should also know

**Do I need a company before raising?**
Yes. Investors buy shares — you need an entity. For Finavig: a UAE
freezone company (e.g. IFZA/Meydan/DIFC, AED 12–30K/yr) is the standard
first step. You can raise via a SAFE before or right after incorporation.

**What is a SAFE?**
"Simple Agreement for Future Equity" — money now, shares later (usually
at the next priced round, at the valuation cap you agreed). It's fast,
cheap (~$0 legal if standard), and the norm at pre-seed in MENA. A
"convertible note" is the older cousin with interest — slightly worse
for you. A "priced round" (selling shares immediately) is for seed+.

**How long does a raise take?**
3–6 months for pre-seed with a first-time solo founder. Budget 60+
investor conversations for 1–3 that actually write a cheque. This is why
Q1 matters: raise when your story is strongest, so the process is shorter.

**How much equity will I give in total across all rounds?**
Founders typically own **50–70% after seed** and **35–55% after Series A**
(with ESOP included). That's normal. Below 30% before Series B = you
raised too much too cheaply.

**What happens if the company fails after raising?**
With a proper limited company and no personal guarantees: investors lose
their money, you owe nothing, the company winds down. Your reputation is
the real cost — GCC investors talk to each other. Fail honestly and
fast, return what's left, and you'll still get meetings next time.

**Should I take one big investor or many small ones?**
One **lead** (takes $100–200K+, sets terms, does the paperwork) + 3–8
angels filling the rest. A round of 20 tiny cheques = a nightmare cap
table for your next raise. Cap your angels at ~10.

**What will investors ask me? (prepare these answers)**
- Who exactly pays, and why now? (GCC SMEs facing fines + cash chaos)
- Why you? (you shipped a full product solo — show it live)
- What's the retention/usage? (honest numbers beat big numbers)
- How big can this get? (payments on renewals → wallet → credit — your
  MONETIZATION.md story)
- What will the money prove in 12 months? (your milestone, in one sentence)

**How much should I pay myself?**
Pre-funding: $0 (you're building an asset). After pre-seed: a modest
founder salary — $1,000–2,000/month in UAE is normal and investors
respect it. A founder paying themselves $10K/month on a $300K raise is a
red flag.

**Do I have to raise at all?**
No. Alternatives, best first:
1. **Grants & equity-free programs** (Hub71, Sheraa, TAQADAM) — money, 0% equity
2. **Revenue** — your freemium tiers (Plus AED 25 / Business AED 99) can fund the app (infra is ~$125/yr!)
3. **Revenue-based financing / SME loans** (Beehive, Liwwa) — repay from revenue, no equity
4. **Competitions** (Beban, Fintech Saudi programs) — prizes + visibility
Finavig's tiny burn means you have real leverage: you can say no to bad terms.

**When should I say NO to money?**
- They want >25% at pre-seed
- They want full board control or a personal guarantee
- They demand a "down round" price later is fine, but a **too-high cap now** is also a trap — it scares the next investor
- They don't understand or care about your market (GCC SMEs) — they'll be noise, not help
- Terms include "participating liquidation preference" or weird clauses — get a lawyer, usually walk

**What is liquidation preference? (simply)**
If the company is sold, investors usually get their money back **first**,
then everyone splits the rest. "1x non-participating" is the standard,
founder-friendly version — accept that. "2x participating" is greedy —
push back.

**What is an ESOP?**
A pool of shares (5–15%) reserved for future hires. Investors will ask
for it. Create it at seed, size it honestly, and remember it dilutes you.

**What's "runway" and how long should it be?**
Runway = months until the money runs out. Raise enough for **12–18
months**. Less than 12 months = you'll be raising again while still
spending — the worst position. Your round size should always equal
"monthly burn × 15".

**Do GCC investors need me to be in their country?**
Hub71 wants you in Abu Dhabi (that's the deal — free money for
commitment). Saudi funds increasingly want a Saudi entity or at least
customers. UAE funds are flexible. Decide based on where your **customers**
and **lead investor** are.

**Taxes?**
UAE: no personal income tax; corporate tax 9% above AED 375K profit
(freezone rules vary). KSA: different regime. Keep clean books from day
one (a part-time accountant costs AED 300–800/month) — investors
diligence this at seed.

## The 5-sentence summary

1. **Don't raise immediately after launch** — launch, get 100–300 users,
   *then* raise; but apply to equity-free programs and accelerators today.
2. **Investment = selling a small piece of the company for cash that goes
   into the company**, plus reporting duties and some rights for them.
3. **Tell them: "$300K on a SAFE at a $3M cap"** (acceptable range
   $2.5–3.5M) — and only after you have usage data.
4. **Pre-seed → seed → Series A/B/C** are levels of proof; you'll dilute
   to ~50–70% ownership after seed, which is normal — 60% of something
   beats 100% of nothing.
5. **Spend on growth first, licences/offices last** — UAE entity now, other
   GCC countries when revenue justifies it; every dirham should make users
   or revenue grow faster.
